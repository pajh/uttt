## Exact-search experiment

We questioned whether the separate exact DFS provides anything the open-ended elapsed-time search cannot. Exact was disabled without source changes by passing `--exact-primary=0 --exact-narrow=0`; bot hello verifies the setting. The resulting one-game instrumented report is `debug-no-exact/report.html`. MM-002 beat orig by 3IAR in 41 plies with zero failures. Timed shallow search independently returned a proved `60000` win on our moves 20 and 21, showing that terminal proofs already propagate through the timed search.

Do not remove exact permanently from this single game. The next useful strength experiment is an exact-enabled versus exact-disabled aggregate with identical settings and seed schedules. Exact-disabled must be positively identified through hello metadata. Its main possible advantage is avoiding a failed exact attempt consuming half the move budget; its possible disadvantage is losing cheap whole-game solutions late in the game.

## Important evaluator finding

The no-exact report showed a selected score of `-701` on our move 19, followed by a proved win on move 20 and the actual 3IAR win on move 21. This is not a report error. Move 19 is global ply 36; its four-ply leaf follows our ply 36, opponent 37, our 38 and opponent 39, ending immediately before our winning ply 40.

At that exact leaf the static strengths were ours 52, opponent 60. The ratio is `10000 * (52-60)/(52+60+2) = -701`. The opponent really was strategically strong: four claimed grids to our three and two surviving master-board routes. However, their last move sent us to a closed grid, giving us unrestricted play, and one legal move immediately claimed the right-middle grid and completed our master-board right column.

The evaluator misses this because `scoreBoard(Board9 board, u16 last_cell, u16 last_bit)` explicitly discards `last_cell` and `last_bit`. It scores general board potential without considering which moves are legal next. This is a narrow, provable horizon error rather than proof that the whole strategic score is bad.

The transposition cache should already be safe for a destination-aware leaf check: `searchKey` includes the complete board, destination `bit`, next player and remaining depth. Verify this carefully before changing scoring. The first implementation should be conservative:

1. At a heuristic leaf, explicitly know `next_player`.
2. Generate that player's legal moves using the stored destination; closed destination grids correctly yield unrestricted play.
3. If any legal move wins the ultimate board immediately, return that player's terminal score.
4. Otherwise retain the current `f1/f2/fc` evaluation unchanged.

This is effectively a targeted one-ply terminal extension. Do not yet score the best ordinary next move or introduce new weights; that would create a broader odd-ply horizon experiment and obscure the simple correctness fix. Add a regression fixture for the ply-39 board from `debug-no-exact/all-moves.csv`: before the extension it evaluates to `-701`; after the extension it must be a proved p0 win. Then regenerate the instrumented report and run the usual 100-game local gate before any longer GitHub comparison.
